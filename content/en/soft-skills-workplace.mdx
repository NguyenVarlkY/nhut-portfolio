# Soft Skills at Work: The Key to an Engineer's Success

Many young engineers focus entirely on the technical side and forget that **90% of the job is working with people**, and only 10% is writing code. Soft skills don't come naturally; they have to be trained deliberately.

## 1. Communication

### 1.1 Explaining complex problems simply
A good engineer must be able to convey complex technical issues to **non-specialists** (stakeholders, PMs, the business).

- Avoid jargon: say "split the system into smaller, manageable parts" instead of "refactor the microservice".
- Use examples, visuals, and real-world comparisons.
- Focus on **outcomes** and **business value**, not technical details.

### 1.2 Written communication
- Write clear tasks with a goal and acceptance criteria.
- Do code reviews constructively.
- Document decisions (ADRs) so future readers understand the "why".

## 2. Proactiveness

- **Take ownership** of work and propose solutions instead of waiting to be assigned.
- When you hit a problem, bring **2-3 options with a recommendation** instead of just asking "how?".
- Update the team on your progress before anyone has to ask.
- Look ahead: anticipate risks and prepare for them.

## 3. Conflict resolution

- Resolve team conflicts in a **civil** way.
- Focus on **the work and the problem**, not on **the person**.
- Listen to and understand the other side before you push back.
- Find common ground: "What's our problem, and what's the best solution for both of us?"
- If you can't resolve it yourselves, talk privately; don't argue in public.

## 4. Time management & perseverance

- Prioritize work by impact and urgency.
- Know when to say "no" to unreasonable requests.
- Stick with long-term goals; don't give up at the first obstacle.

> *"Soft skills are the hardest to learn, but they pay the most."*

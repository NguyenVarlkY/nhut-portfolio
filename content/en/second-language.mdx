# Learning Japanese, Korean, German, or French: A Competitive Edge

English is the foundation, but once your technical skills are solid, learning **a second foreign language** can make you a **"rare"** candidate for international projects.

## 1. Why learn a second language?

### 1.1 Niche job markets
- **Japanese:** Vietnam has many Japanese companies (offshore, outsourcing). Engineers who speak Japanese earn significantly more.
- **Korean:** Samsung, LG, and many other Korean conglomerates in Vietnam always need Korean-speaking engineers.
- **German:** Many German companies outsource software development to Vietnam (offshore).
- **French:** Projects in partnership with France, Belgium, and Canada (Quebec).

### 1.2 A competitive edge
- Plenty of engineers speak English, but far fewer combine **Japanese + technical skills**.
- You can talk to clients directly, without going through a translator.
- Companies are willing to pay a **premium** for this skill.

## 2. Which language should you learn?

### 2.1 By opportunity
- **Japanese:** The most opportunities in Vietnam, especially in outsourcing and manufacturing.
- **Korean:** Big opportunities in Korean companies, especially electronics and manufacturing.
- **German:** Opportunities in technical, engineering, and automation companies.
- **French:** Opportunities in legal, finance, and insurance projects.

### 2.2 How to choose
- **By career goal:** Which countries does your target company work with?
- **By interest:** Which language do you enjoy and can you keep up with?
- **Pick one language;** don't spread yourself across several at once.

## 3. An effective learning strategy

### 3.1 Learn systematically
- Start with the writing system and basic grammar.
- Aim for JLPT N3 (Japanese), TOPIK 3 (Korean), TestDaF B1 (German), or DELF B1 (French).
- Practice all four skills: listening, speaking, reading, and writing.

### 3.2 Learn through a technical context
- Read technical docs and watch coding videos in that language.
- Join that country's developer communities.
- Do small projects with international clients.

### 3.3 Be persistent and consistent
- 30-60 minutes a day, every day.
- Mix it into your work: use Japanese for notes, variable names, or comments.
- When it gets hard, remember that **a second language + technical skills** is a rare and valuable combination.

> *"English keeps you from falling behind. A second language helps you pull ahead."*

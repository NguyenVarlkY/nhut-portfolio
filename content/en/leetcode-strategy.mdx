# Conquering LeetCode

LeetCode isn't about memorizing answers. It's about building **algorithmic thinking**, a key skill for getting through the technical interview rounds at big companies.

## 1. How to practice

### 1.1 Pattern recognition
Don't solve problems at random. Learn by **pattern**:
- **Two Pointers:** For sorted arrays and pair-finding problems.
- **Sliding Window:** For substring/subarray problems with a condition.
- **Graph (BFS/DFS):** For pathfinding and connectivity problems.
- **Dynamic Programming:** For optimization problems with overlapping subproblems.
- **Binary Search:** For searching in sorted data.

### 1.2 Time boxing
- Don't spend more than **45 minutes** on a single problem.
- If you're stuck, look at a **hint** or read the **solution**.
- Then **re-code it from scratch** a day later to test your memory and deepen your understanding.

### 1.3 Complexity analysis
Always ask yourself: "Can I do better?"
- Analyze the **time complexity** (O(n), O(n²), O(log n)...).
- Analyze the **space complexity**.
- Weigh the trade-off between time and memory.

## 2. Effective practice strategies

- **Spaced repetition:** Revisit solved problems after 1 day, 3 days, and 1 week.
- **Study by topic:** Spend 1-2 weeks on one pattern before moving on to the next.
- **Have a target:** 5-10 problems a week, consistently, beats grinding one day and quitting.
- **Take notes:** Write down the idea, the approach, and the lesson for each problem.

## 3. Interview mindset

### 3.1 Clarification
Always clarify:
- **Input/output:** Data types and size limits.
- **Edge cases:** Empty input, a single element, duplicates, negative numbers.
- **Constraints:** Can you use extra memory? Can you reorder the input?

### 3.2 Brute force first
Present a **simple solution first** (brute force), then optimize.
- Presenting the brute force shows you understand the problem.
- Analyzing and optimizing step by step shows how you think.

### 3.3 Clean code
- Use clear, meaningful variable names.
- **Modularize** your code into small, readable functions.
- Walk through examples and edge cases before you finish.

> *"Good engineers are not the ones who know every algorithm by heart, but the ones who can derive the right approach under pressure."*

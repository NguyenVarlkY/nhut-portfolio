# Foreign Languages: A Career "Weapon"

In IT, foreign languages open the door to working on global systems. It's a "soft" skill, but it has a huge impact on your salary and growth opportunities.

## 1. English: a mandatory foundation

English is the **official language** of the tech industry. Most documentation, source code, and technical discussion happen in English.

### 1.1 B1: enough to work
At **B1+**, you can:
- **Read documentation** and technical specs.
- **Communicate at a basic level** in day-to-day work.
- Write emails, code comments, and bug reports.

### 1.2 C1: to widen your options
At **C1+**, you can:
- Take part in **international interviews** at foreign companies.
- Work directly with international clients and teams.
- Land **freelance** work at much higher rates (10-20 USD/hour even for juniors).

### 1.3 How to improve your English
- Read English technical docs and blogs every day.
- Listen to podcasts and watch technical videos without subtitles.
- Write blog posts and technical docs in English.
- Join international communities (Stack Overflow, GitHub discussions).
- Talk with people from other countries on freelance platforms.

## 2. A language-learning strategy for engineers

- **Learn in context:** Pick up domain vocabulary while you work instead of memorizing isolated words.
- **Prioritize speaking and listening:** At work, communication matters more than perfect grammar.
- **Practice every day:** 30 minutes a day beats 3 hours on the weekend.
- **Set concrete goals:** "Read the Next.js docs without a dictionary" instead of "get good at English".

> *"English isn't a side skill; it's the gatekeeper to the best opportunities. Without it, you only see a small slice of the global market."*
